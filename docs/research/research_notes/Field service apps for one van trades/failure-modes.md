## Key question 1 — Trade no-show or lateness: what good practice looks like, who does it, and what UK consumers expect

### Takeaway
UK regulated industries have converged on the same shape: a bounded arrival window (not a time), a day-before reminder, an on-the-day "on my way" message with a live map, and an automatic, fixed, inflation-indexed payment if the window is missed or cancelled with under 24 hours' notice (£32.31 telecoms, £30 energy). Nobody in the regulated sector escalates to a phone call with a recorded message; they rely on automatic credit plus a rebook path. Consumers' strongest complaint is not lateness itself but silence: a fifth-to-a-third of homeowners report a late or absent tradesperson at their last visit.

### Cited Findings

**Telecoms (Openreach-based ISPs and Virgin Media): windows, notice, and automatic compensation**
- Under Ofcom's Automatic Compensation scheme, as last checked September 2026, a missed scheduled appointment pays **£32.31** "if your provider's designated engineer cancels or misses an appointment with less than 24 hours' notice"; a delayed repair pays **£10.34 per day** after 2 working days; a delayed start of a new service pays **£6.46 per day**. "These figures are updated annually, based on inflation." Compensation is applied as a bill credit, "usually ... within 30 days." Participating providers: BT, EE, Hyperoptic, Plusnet, Sky (including NOW Broadband), TalkTalk, Utility Warehouse, Virgin Media, Vodafone, Zen. — [broadband.co.uk guide to automatic compensation](https://www.broadband.co.uk/broadband/help/guide-automatic-broadband-landline-compensation) (Ofcom's own page [automatic-compensation-need-know](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/service-quality/automatic-compensation-need-know/) returned HTTP 403 to my fetcher; search summaries of it gave the same £32.31 figure and a payment deadline of "no later than 30 calendar days after the date of the missed appointment").
- Earlier indexed values of the same payment, useful to show the annual uplift: £26.24 (2022) — [ISPreview, June 2022](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2022/06/25-missed-appointment-fee-angers-virgin-media-uk-customers.html); £31.19 (BT's stated figure, Nov 2025) — [ISPreview, Nov 2025](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/11/broadband-isp-bt-ponders-possible-50-charge-for-missed-uk-appointments.html).
- Openreach's own customer guidance: the engineer will "Arrive within the confirmed time slot from your provider"; the customer must make sure "someone aged 18 or over is at home for the full appointment window"; if nobody is home you rebook through the provider and "charges may apply for missed appointments." The page says nothing about a card being left or about on-the-way tracking. — [Openreach: what will happen during my repair visit](https://www.openreach.com/help-and-support/what-will-happen-during-my-repair-visit)
- Typical Openreach-based slot definitions as published by ISPs: "Morning (08:00-13:00) or Evening (13:00-18:00)", "a representative aged over 18 must stay for the whole slot" — [XLN broadband installation help](https://help.xln.co.uk/en/articles/10031980-broadband-installation); TalkTalk describes appointments "between 7am-6pm Monday to Saturday, with a 2-3 hour timeslot, and the engineer can arrive at any time within it" — [TalkTalk community article](https://community.talktalk.co.uk/t5/Articles/Your-TalkTalk-engineer-appointment/ta-p/2205431).
- BT's broadband terms require the customer to give "at least two working days" notice to change or cancel an appointment; a leaked Nov 2025 internal email floated a possible £50 customer no-show charge "to encourage customers to re-book appointments if they cannot meet it", with BT saying it does not currently plan to enforce it. The same leak alleged "about 10% of fibre (FTTC/P/SOGEA) installs are missed on the day by customers, despite reminders", and credited Openreach with reducing missed appointments by "improving direct customer communications." — [ISPreview, Nov 2025](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/11/broadband-isp-bt-ponders-possible-50-charge-for-missed-uk-appointments.html)

**Energy suppliers and network operators: the Guaranteed Standards**
- The Electricity and Gas (Standards of Performance) (Suppliers) Regulations 2015: reg 3(3) requires the supplier to offer an appointment slot "no more than 4 hours long" "during working hours"; reg 3(5)-(6) says a customer's request for a specific slot must not be unreasonably refused unless the slot is "less than 2 hours long" or outside working hours; reg 3(7) "A supplier must not rearrange an appointment less than 1 working day prior to the date of the appointment without" the customer's express, recorded agreement; reg 8(2) a failure triggers "a payment of £30 (a 'standard payment')" within 10 working days; reg 8(3) if that is late, a further £30 "additional standard payment". — [legislation.gov.uk, SI 2015/1544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1544/made)
- Enforcement example: British Gas paid £1.1m after missing appointments and then failing to pay the £30 within 10 days; the package was "£30 for initial failed appointment and £30 for not paying customers within the required 10 days, plus an extra £30 to each affected customer." — [Ofgem press release](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/publications/british-gas-pays-ps11m-compensate-customers-after-agents-missed-appointments)
- Distribution network operators' GSOP payments are now indexed annually: 2026/27 amounts are "calculated by adjusting the values on or after 1 April 2025 by the percentage change in the Consumer Prices Index" (CPIH) and licensees "must put these into place by 1 April 2026." The actual £ figures sit in a linked PDF/XLSX I could not fetch. — [Ofgem: Adjustments to the Guaranteed Standards of Performance 2026](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/guidance/adjustments-guaranteed-standards-performance-2026)
- Some suppliers pay above the statutory floor: OVO's community forum states a "standard £40 compensation known as Guaranteed Standards of Performance (GSOP) payment" when an engineer fails to attend without 24 hours' notice. (Forum source, supplier-specific.) — [OVO forum](https://forum.ovoenergy.com/my-account-140/when-will-i-receive-my-compensation-for-a-missed-engineer-appointment-1136/index5.html)
- The Financial Ombudsman has considered claims for more than the £30 standard payment where a customer lost earnings; in one decision British Gas said "the £30 it already paid Mr C is the standard level of compensation it pays to customers in this situation", and the Ombudsman noted it has "no power to require British Gas to change its appointment system or to punish it", only to award fair compensation. — [FOS decision DRN8121576](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN8121576.pdf); [FOS decision DRN9002544](https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN9002544.pdf)

**Delivery: DPD, Amazon, Royal Mail**
- DPD Predict gives "a one hour delivery window, notified by SMS and email"; Follow My Parcel lets receivers "watch the progress of their delivery on a real-time map, all the way down to a final 15 minute timeslot." — [DPD UK: Predict & Follow My Parcel](https://www.dpd.co.uk/content/products_services/predict_fmp.jsp)
- The DPD Predict flow: delivery date notified when the parcel reaches DPD, one-hour slot named on the day, live tracking during delivery, and the parcel can be "redirected online up to 15 minutes before final handover" to another date, a neighbour, a safe place, or a pickup shop. — [DPD Switzerland: Predict](https://www.dpd.com/ch/en/predict)
- Follow My Parcel launched in 2013 with ASOS as launch customer. — [CEP-Research](https://www.cep-research.com/?p=43935)
- Amazon Map Tracking shows the driver on a map and "the number of stops between the driver and your home" on delivery day; the map appears only "once the driver has 10 or fewer stops remaining"; only for Amazon's own network. — [EcommerceBytes, May 2018](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2018/05/28/amazon-improves-package-tracking-capabilities/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/1289417/amazon-is-adding-live-maps-to-its-package-tracking-services)
- Royal Mail's model after a failed attempt is a card plus automatic retry: the postie "will leave a Something for You card which confirms the postie will automatically attempt to redeliver the next working day", with online redelivery needing "48 working hours' notice", free redirection to a neighbour/shop/locker in the postcode area, and 18 calendar days before return to sender. — [Royal Mail auto-redelivery](https://www.royalmail.com/auto-redelivery); [Royal Mail redelivery](https://www.royalmail.com/personal/receiving-mail/redelivery)

**Ride-hailing: lateness defined in minutes against an ETA**
- Uber waives the rider's cancellation fee "if your Driver exceeds the initial estimated time of arrival (ETA) by 5 minutes, if your Driver hasn't made any progress towards your location or if your Driver is driving further away from the pick-up location." — [Uber UK blog: changes to cancellation fees](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/changes-to-cancellation-fees)
- From 16 November 2023 in London "your trip will no longer be automatically cancelled after 8 minutes (for UberX as an example)" of rider no-show; the driver decides whether to wait. — [Uber UK blog: changes to wait times](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/changes-to-wait-times)
- Third-party fee guide (Oct 2025): UberX in London has "a 2-minute grace period" before wait-time charges and a £4.90 cancellation fee. — [TaxiPoint](https://www.taxi-point.co.uk/post/uber-cancellation-and-wait-time-fees-in-london-what-they-cost-and-why-it-s-charged)
- Uber's ETA engineering: DeepETA replaced gradient-boosted trees because scaling XGBoost "became untenable", and the model "must return an ETA within a few milliseconds." — [Uber Engineering: DeepETA](https://www.uber.com/blog/deepeta-how-uber-predicts-arrival-times/)

**NHS: reminders and the wording that changes behaviour**
- About 5.5 million outpatient appointments in England were missed in 2012-13 (about 9.3%); each costs the NHS £160. In a randomised trial of 10,111 patients, a reminder stating the specific cost of a missed appointment produced an 8.4% DNA rate vs 11.1% for the existing message; "Mentioning costs in general terms was significantly less effective than stating the exact amount"; all messages included a call-to-cancel instruction. — [Warwick Business School summary](https://www.wbs.ac.uk/news/how-to-stop-people-missing-hospital-appointments/); primary paper [PMC4569397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569397)
- Two-way SMS where the patient replies to cancel or rebook: Brighton's trust reported missed appointments cut "by a phenomenal 30 per cent" — [Brighton & Hove News](https://brightonandhovenews.org/?p=52854); Barts reported falls of "more than 20%" in some services — [Digital Health, 2012](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2012/01/barts-cuts-dnas-with-text-service/); BT's healthcare case study uses "To confirm reply 1, to reschedule reply 2 and to cancel reply 3" and claims a 6% DNA reduction (vendor figure). — [BT 2-way messaging healthcare use case (PDF)](https://business.bt.com/content/dam/bt-business/pdfs/corporate/products/enabling-your-people/bt-2-way-messaging-healthcare-use-case.pdf)

**What UK consumers experience and expect from tradespeople**
- Markel Direct / Censuswide, fieldwork April 2026, 500 UK homeowners and 500 tradespeople: 35% of homeowners call tradespeople failing to turn up a "major inconvenience", 25% cite cancelling at short notice, 19% cite arriving late; regionally the share who experienced a no-show ranges from 10% (North West) to 31% (Yorkshire and the Humber); 72% of homeowners say they are home at the agreed time; tradespeople's second-biggest wish (45%) is "the homeowner being home and on time". — [Markel: tradie etiquette UK](https://www.markeluk.com/knowledge-centre/tradie-etiquette-uk)
- HomeServe research: 36% of Britons said the last tradesperson was late and one in 100 did not show at all. — [HomeServe via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/waiting-in-drain-homeserve-discover-brits-spend-72m-hours-a-year-waiting-for-plumbers-201574671.html)
- Fair Fix (2023, 1,609 respondents): 45% waited 1-5 working days for a call-out to arrive. — [London Loves Business](https://londonlovesbusiness.com/45-of-people-wait-up-to-five-days-for-a-tradesperson-call-out/)
- Citizens Advice analysis of 1,000 broadband problems found people "had to repeatedly negotiate with providers to get engineers out or receive any kind of financial compensation", and 55% of those with a problem did not seek redress. — [Citizens Advice press release](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/citizens-advice-responds-to-ofcom-consultation/)

**Field-service products: what "running late" messaging actually is today**
- Housecall Pro: "A text message will be sent when you click the 'On my way' button"; it is SMS only, not email. — [Housecall Pro help: job notifications](https://help.housecallpro.com/notifications/what-job-notifications-will-my-customers-receive). With the Vehicle GPS add-on, "The Customer On My Way text message now has a link to view the driver's location", and "The customer map link will expire after you or your tech pushes Start, Finish, Invoice/Pay, or after 2 hours (whichever comes first)." — [Housecall Pro: Vehicle GPS tracking overview](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6060351-vehicle-gps-tracking-overview)
- Jobber: "with one click in the Jobber mobile app, you can send a standardized text message letting customers know you're on the way (or running late)" — manual, not automatic. — [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/?p=55)
- Vendor descriptions of automated lateness escalation exist (threshold "typically 15-30 minutes", revised ETA from "current crew location, remaining job duration, and travel time to the next appointment") but only from marketing pages. — [Fieldproxy: notify customer crew running late](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/automations/window-crew-running-late-notify)

### Inferences
- The regulated UK norm is a *window* plus a *fixed consequence*, not a precise time. A one-person trade cannot afford to copy £30 automatic credits, but it can copy the structure: a window the customer can see, a visible "window passed" state, and a pre-agreed gesture (an apology message, a discount, or a free re-visit) that fires without the customer having to ask. The Citizens Advice 55%-no-redress figure is the reason to make it automatic: customers mostly do not chase.
- Nobody in the regulated or delivery sectors uses an escalation ladder ending in a phone call with a recorded message. The observed ladders are: (1) day-before reminder with reply-to-cancel, (2) on-the-day slot or "on my way" with a map, (3) a state change plus automatic credit when the window is breached. For TurnUp, an honest ladder is: push → SMS fallback if the push is unacknowledged (iOS web push is not guaranteed delivery, see KQ3) → the trade's own phone call. Automated voice is unusual in this sector and would read as impersonal from a one-person business.
- Uber's "ETA exceeded by 5 minutes" and the vendor "15-30 minute" thresholds suggest a two-level lateness definition: *drift* (ETA slipped a few minutes, update silently) and *breach* (window end passed with no "On site", notify the customer proactively and offer options). The NHS trial suggests the breach message should be concrete ("Will is 25 minutes behind; new arrival 10:25-10:45") rather than generic ("running late").
- The Markel finding that tradespeople's own top operational wish is "the homeowner being home and on time" (45%) means the product should treat the two no-show directions symmetrically; the customer's "I'm running late myself" button (KQ6) is as valuable to the trade as the trade's "on my way" is to the customer.

### Gaps
- Ofcom's own page could not be fetched (HTTP 403); the £32.31 / 30-day figures come from a reseller guide checked September 2026 and from search summaries of the Ofcom page.
- I found no published Openreach or utility policy describing a staged *escalation* (push → SMS → call) when an engineer is late inside the window; what is documented is the post-hoc compensation. I found no UK survey quantifying how many minutes late a consumer considers acceptable before wanting a message.
- The exact 2026/27 DNO missed-appointment payment sits in an Ofgem PDF/XLSX my fetcher did not open.

## Key question 2 — Customer not home: the "sorry we missed you" pattern, missed-visit fees, and what UK law allows

### Takeaway
Carriers have moved from paper cards to photo-plus-timestamp evidence and an automatic retry or redirect; UK trade guidance is a card plus a timestamped message and a short wait, with any no-access fee enforceable only if agreed before the booking, proportionate to actual loss, and not hidden. The legal frame is the Consumer Rights Act 2015 unfair-terms test and, for cancellation rights, the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 with its urgent-repairs exception.

### Cited Findings

**Industry "missed you" patterns**
- DPD (Belgium FAQ, same group process): the driver takes a photo as extra proof of delivery, the recipient can refuse the photo and sign instead, photos are kept for 3 months; if the neighbour is also out "the parcel goes to a nearby Pickup shop, and a note is left in the letterbox with the address and collection date." — [DPD BE FAQ](https://www.dpd.com/be/en/contact/faq/receiving-parcels/?post=21). A UK retailer notes DPD UK no longer leaves a card and retries; it takes the parcel to the nearest pickup shop unless instructed otherwise. — [Tony's Chocolonely UK delivery changes](https://tonyschocolonely.com/uk/en/delivery-changes-dpd)
- Royal Mail: card left, automatic redelivery next working day, online rebooking with 48 working hours' notice, 18-day hold. — [Royal Mail auto-redelivery](https://www.royalmail.com/auto-redelivery)
- Amazon Photo on Delivery photographs packages left unattended; "The photo will focus on the placement of the package"; shoppers can turn the feature off. — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2018/05/28/amazon-improves-package-tracking-capabilities/)
- Photo proof is contested when it does not show the right place: Resolver documents Evri photos "that didn't match their delivery location", and consumer guidance says "a photograph does not automatically mean a successful delivery" and you can dispute if it does not show the nominated place. — [Resolver](https://news.resolver.co.uk/resolver-stories-evri-courier-chaos/); [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/news/what-are-your-consumer-rights-for-late-or-missing-parcels/amp). Conversely, a carrier claims process treats "tracking shows delivery with an End of Life (EOL) scan, GEO location, and photo evidence" as sufficient to reject a loss claim. — [Shippo: Evri claims](https://support.goshippo.com/hc/en-us/articles/7711950911259-Submitting-Shipping-Claims-For-Evri-Shipments)
- Social landlord practice: North Lanarkshire issues two "no access" cards before cancelling a repair; an emergency repair may be cancelled immediately "with the resident liable for the cost of this premium call out." — [North Lanarkshire Council](https://northlanarkshire.gov.uk/housing/repairs-and-advice/what-happens-if-i-miss-appointment)

**UK trade guidance on no-access**
- Trade2base: "Leave a calling card and send a confirmation message so there's a timestamped record"; log date/time and reason (no answer, tenant out, no key); day-before confirmation and "a follow-up call if there's no reply"; "Don't stand on the doorstep trying to phone the customer for ten minutes while your next appointment slips"; "A no-access fee is only enforceable if the customer agreed to it before the job"; suggested amounts £30-£60 (local domestic), £45-£90 (half call-out), £60-£120+ (specialist/distance); example trigger wording "if we cannot gain access at the agreed time and you have not given us at least 24 hours' notice"; waive for loyal customers and genuine emergencies, apply to repeat no-shows; for landlord work "Make sure your terms with letting agents spell out who pays for tenant no-access." Photos are not mentioned. — [Trade2base: no access](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-no-access-uk)
- Law-firm guidance: cancellation/no-show clauses must be "transparent and fair, not hidden in small print or drafted in a way that creates a significant imbalance against the customer", "brought to the customer's attention before the booking is made, and proportionate to the loss or cost." — [Sprintlaw UK: booking terms for home services](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/booking-terms-for-uk-home-services-businesses/)
- Utility precedent for charging the customer: Virgin Media's £25 one-off charge "if their engineers show up for a scheduled appointment" and nobody answers; customers "usually forewarned about the £25 charge" at booking; VMO2: "Charges for missed appointments are common across the industry"; at the time Virgin paid £26.24 under Ofcom's scheme for its own misses, and readers criticised the asymmetry ("when they fail to make an appointment they should equally reimburse your wasted time"). — [ISPreview, June 2022](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2022/06/25-missed-appointment-fee-angers-virgin-media-uk-customers.html)
- A UK parliamentary petition argued tenants who take unpaid leave for a missed appointment have no recourse for lost earnings. — [petition.parliament.uk 259709](https://petition.parliament.uk/archived/petitions/259709)

**The law: Consumer Rights Act 2015 unfair terms**
- Schedule 2 "grey list": para 4 — a term "permitting the trader to retain sums paid by the consumer where the consumer decides not to conclude or perform the contract" without an equivalent payment "where the trader is the party cancelling the contract"; para 6 — a term "requiring a consumer who fails to fulfil his obligations under the contract to pay a disproportionately high sum"; para 11 — "enabling the trader to alter the terms of the contract unilaterally without a valid reason". — [CRA 2015 Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/schedule/2)
- GOV.UK consumer guide: a business may keep a deposit or charge a cancellation fee only "if the contract term is fair" and a charge "is not fair just because it's in the contract you signed"; it is "only entitled to keep or receive an amount sufficient to cover their actual losses"; "Businesses must take reasonable steps to reduce their losses" e.g. by re-selling the slot; "Non-refundable deposits should only be a small percentage of the total price"; cancellation charges "must be a genuine estimate of the business' direct loss"; "Businesses cannot rely on unfair terms." — [GOV.UK: Cancelling goods or services](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cancelling-goods-or-services-guide-for-consumers/cancelling-goods-or-services)
- CMA: unfair terms include those that "allow a business to take a large, upfront deposit and refuse to refund any of the customer's money if they cancel"; an unfair term "can't be enforced against the customer even if they have signed up to it." — [CMA blog, 2019](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2019/06/27/holiday-deposits-and-cancellation-fees-are-you-being-treated-fairly/)
- Citizens Advice: ask the business "to explain how they calculated the amount"; an unfair term is not binding and the consumer may resist paying. — [Citizens Advice consumer information note (PDF)](https://citizensadvice.org.uk/Global/CitizensAdvice/campaigns/ncw15/ncw15-Consumerinformationnote.pdf)

**The law: Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013**
- Regulation 30: 14-day cancellation period for distance and off-premises contracts; regulation 31: extended by up to 12 months if the trader fails to give the Schedule 2 cancellation information; regulation 34: refund within 14 days. — [SI 2013/3134 Part 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/part/3); [Explanatory note](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/note)
- Regulation 28(1)(e): Part 3 does not apply to "contracts where the consumer has specifically requested a visit from the trader for the purpose of carrying out urgent repairs or maintenance"; but reg 28(2) keeps the cancellation rules for "services in addition to the urgent repairs or maintenance requested" and "goods other than replacement parts necessarily used in making the repairs" supplied on that visit. — [SI 2013/3134 reg 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28/made)
- Regulation 36: "The trader must not begin the supply of a service before the end of the cancellation period" unless the consumer makes an express request, which for an off-premises contract must be on a durable medium; if the consumer then cancels they pay "in proportion to what has been supplied, in comparison with the full coverage of the contract"; if the trader failed to give the cancellation or cost information, "The consumer bears no cost for supply of the service, in full or in part." — [SI 2013/3134 reg 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36/made)
- Citizens Advice plain-English version: the 14-day cooling-off period applies to services arranged online, by phone or by mail order, and to off-premises sales where "the service costs £42 or more"; it starts "the day after you enter a contract"; no cooling-off period if arranged at the business's premises; if you asked for the service to start in the period you pay for what was provided. — [Citizens Advice: cancelling a service you've arranged](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/cancelling-a-service-youve-arranged/)

### Inferences
- A product-grade "sorry we missed you" record for a trade is: arrival timestamp, device location at arrival, a photo of the front door (not the interior, and composed so the house is identifiable: the Evri disputes are about photos that could be anywhere), the attempts made (knocked, rang the number at hh:mm, message sent at hh:mm), how long waited, and a rebook link. That is the same evidence set a carrier uses to defeat a "never came" claim, and it is what makes a no-access fee defensible.
- A no-access fee survives the CRA 2015 test only if it was shown and agreed before the booking (the booking confirmation is the natural "durable medium"), is a genuine pre-estimate of direct loss (the lost slot, travel), and is mirrored: if the trade misses the window, the customer gets something of equal value (Schedule 2 para 4). A fee the customer can avoid by cancelling with notice (24 hours in trade guidance; Virgin and the Ofcom scheme also use 24 hours; BT uses two working days) reads as fair; a flat fee with no notice route reads as para 6.
- Because reg 36 makes a service started within 14 days of an online/phone booking chargeable only on an express request, and because an "emergency" visit falls under reg 28(1)(e) only for the urgent repair itself, the booking confirmation should carry (a) the cancellation information, (b) an express "please start before the 14 days are up" acknowledgement, and (c) the no-access and short-notice-cancellation terms, in that order. Missing (a) voids the right to charge for work done in the period at all.
- The "wait" duration is nowhere specified in law; trade guidance says do not let the next appointment slip, carriers do not wait at all. Ten minutes with two contact attempts, logged, is a defensible default; what matters legally is the record, not the duration.

### Gaps
- I found no UK court or ombudsman decision ruling on a tradesperson's no-access fee specifically; enforceability is inferred from the CRA 2015 grey list, GOV.UK and CMA guidance.
- Evri's and DPD UK's current card wording and whether an attempted-delivery photo is shown to the recipient in the UK app: not confirmed from a primary UK source.
- Whether Virgin Media's £25 fee is still charged in 2026: not confirmed.

## Key question 3 — Proof of attendance without signal: geofencing on iOS and Android, offline-first queues, how field apps reconstruct check-in times, and what a PWA can and cannot do

### Takeaway
Native region monitoring is designed for exactly this: iOS and Android both wake a non-running app on a boundary crossing, both want a radius of about 100-200 m, both deliver with minutes of latency, and both degrade badly without Wi-Fi in rural areas. ServiceM8, Jobber and Timeero all accept that and record the *detected* arrival time while asking the user to confirm later. A web app gets none of the background machinery: no background geolocation, no Background Sync on Safari, push only when installed to the Home Screen, so a PWA must capture location at the moment of a user tap and queue it locally.

### Cited Findings

**iOS Core Location [native only]**
- "Core Location limits to 20 the number of regions that may be simultaneously monitored by a single app." — [Apple Location Awareness PG: Region Monitoring (archived)](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/LocationAwarenessPG/RegionMonitoring/RegionMonitoring.html)
- "In iOS, regions associated with your app are tracked at all times, including when the app isn't running. If a region boundary is crossed while an app isn't running, that app is relaunched into the background to handle the event. Similarly, if the app is suspended when the event occurs, it's woken up and given a short amount of time (around 10 seconds) to handle the event." — same source
- Debounce built in: "the user's location must cross the region boundary, move away from the boundary by a minimum distance, and remain at that minimum distance for at least 20 seconds before the notifications are reported"; "if Wi-Fi is disabled, region monitoring is significantly less accurate ... for testing purposes, you can assume that the minimum distance is approximately 200 meters"; "The system doesn't report boundary crossings until the boundary plus a system-defined cushion distance is exceeded." Radius above `maximumRegionMonitoringDistance` fails registration. — same source
- Significant-change service: "delivers updates only when there has been a significant change in the device's location, such as 500 meters or more"; "can use Wi-Fi to determine the user's position and consumes the least amount of power"; if the app is suspended or terminated "the service automatically wakes up your app when new location data arrives" with "around 10 seconds" to act; requires `kCLAuthorizationStatusAuthorizedAlways`; "When a user disables the Background App Refresh setting either globally or for your app, the significant-change location service doesn't relaunch your app." Continuous `startUpdatingLocation` in the background needs the `location` UIBackgroundModes key; significant-change and region monitoring do not. — [Apple Location Awareness PG: Core Location (archived)](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/LocationAwarenessPG/CoreLocation/CoreLocation.html)
- Apple DTS on the developer forums: the low-energy APIs (significant change, region monitoring) do not need the `location` background mode, "All the other APIs will require 'Always' location authorization", and the app may be relaunched without the `.location` launch option being visible, so do not rely on it to detect a background launch. App Review has asked developers to drop the `location` background mode in favour of significant-change or region monitoring when continuous updates are not needed. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 818370](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/818370)
- Field report: iOS accepts small radii "but reliability drops quickly below about 100 meters." (Third-party.) — [Capgo](https://capgo.app/blog/capacitor-geofences-not-triggering-ios/)
- `CLVisit` exists as a separate, lower-power arrival/departure service: the archived table lists `CLVisit` as "Requires location updates in background mode: No / Can use location updates in background mode: Yes". — [Apple Core Location guide, Table 1-1](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/LocationAwarenessPG/CoreLocation/CoreLocation.html)

**Android Geofencing API [native only]**
- "You can have multiple active geofences, with a limit of 100 per app, per device user." — [Android developers: Create and monitor geofences](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing)
- Radius: "set a minimum radius of 100 meters. This helps account for the location accuracy of typical Wi-Fi networks"; "the minimum radius of the geofence should be set between 100 - 150 meters. When Wi-Fi is available location accuracy is usually between 20 - 50 meters"; "When Wi-Fi location isn't available (for example, when you are driving in rural areas) the location accuracy degrades. The accuracy range can be as large as several hundred meters to several kilometers. In cases like this, you should create geofences using a larger radius." — same source
- Dwell: use `GEOFENCE_TRANSITION_DWELL` instead of ENTER so "the dwelling alert is sent only when the user stops inside a geofence for a given period of time", set by a loitering delay; this reduces "alert spam". — same source
- Latency: "Usually the latency is less than 2 minutes, even less when the device has been moving. If Background Location Limits are in effect, the latency is about 2-3 minutes on average. If the device has been stationary for a significant period of time, the latency may increase (up to 6 minutes)." On Android 8.0+ a background app "responds to geofencing events every couple of minutes." — same source
- Fragility: "On most devices, the geofence service uses only network location for geofence triggering"; "if Wi-Fi is turned off, your application might never get geofence alerts"; geofences must be re-registered after reboot, reinstall, app data clear, Play services data clear, or a `GEOFENCE_NOT_AVAILABLE` alert. — same source

Once ServiceM8 decides to act, the recorded time of that event will be rolled back to the time you actually arrived or left the location"; if it could not check you in, tapping Start job "lets you wind the check-in time back to when it detected your arrival"; on clock-off it "suggests earlier clock-off times based on your movements"; every automatic action can be undone "by tapping its push notification"; requires "Always Allow" with "Precise Location"; it "will take the job's address into account" and may fail if the address was not selected from the dropdown; the app does not share location on Break or Clocked Off. — [ServiceM8: How Automated Time Tracking Works](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works); [ServiceM8 update: check-in time adjustment](https://www.servicem8.com/updates/check-in-time-adjustment)

- Housecall Pro: "a more frequent location check happens once a job is marked 'started'"; tracking is phone-location based. — [Housecall Pro help: GPS](https://help.housecallpro.com/articles/362425-gps-how-to-track-your-employees) (page returned 404 to my fetcher; detail from the search summary)
- Enterprise precedent for the offline case: ServiceNow Field Service "Offline check-ins skip the geofence check and are flagged as 'Geofence could not be validated.'" — [ServiceNow docs: onsite arrival and check-in](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/IVKDA~MgX_6UMb0D9l5eLg/Q3s_ccTVKLFaJ~~yAOmK1A)

**What a web app / PWA can and cannot do**
- Web Push on iOS: added in iOS/iPadOS 16.4 for "Home Screen web apps" only; "A web app that has been added to the Home Screen can request permission to receive push notifications"; the manifest needs `display` of standalone or fullscreen; permission must be requested "in response to direct user interaction"; Badging API supported. The post says nothing about background sync or background geolocation. — [WebKit blog: Web Push for Web Apps on iOS and iPadOS](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)
- Unlike Chrome/Firefox/Edge, Safari "allows push permission requests only from sites bookmarked to the home screen." — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-web-push-is-now-supported-on-safari); one guide reports Home Screen web app push "is not available in EU countries on iOS 17.4 and later". — [MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide) (not verified against Apple; check current EU status)

- A developer reported an installed PWA returning "location is denied" on iOS 26.0.1 while the same page worked in Safari. Outcome of the thread unknown. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 804381](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804381)

### Inferences
- For TurnUp as a web app, the achievable proof-of-attendance is **tap-time evidence**: when the trade taps "On site", capture `navigator.geolocation.getCurrentPosition` with high accuracy, the device clock, and the server-received time, store it in IndexedDB, and retry upload on `online`/visibility events (Background Sync only on Chromium; on iOS the retry must happen when the PWA is next foregrounded). Record three timestamps per event: device time at tap, GPS fix time, server receipt time; a gap between the first two and the third is the offline interval and is itself evidence, not a defect.
- A PWA cannot do what ServiceM8 does (wake on arrival and back-date). The nearest web equivalent is a *prompted* reconstruction: when the trade taps "On site" late, show "You have been near this address since about 09:04 (from your last fix). Use 09:04?" This only works if the page took a fix earlier (for example at "On my way" tap, or if the trade left the tab in the foreground on a dash mount). The ServiceM8 "wind back" and Timeero "clock-in time reflects actual entry" behaviours show the industry already treats the detected time, not the confirmed time, as the record, with the tap being confirmation.
- If TurnUp ever ships a native shell, the right primitives are region monitoring on a 150-200 m radius around the job address (iOS 20-region cap is irrelevant for one van; register the day's jobs), Android `DWELL` with a 1-2 minute loitering delay, and both platforms' "Always" permission. Expect 2-6 minutes of latency and expect rural failure without Wi-Fi: both vendors say so. Design the UI so the geofence is a *suggestion* with a visible "we think" and a one-tap correction, as Jobber's reminder mode and ServiceM8's undo-by-notification do.
- Jobber's own admission that location timers "require an active internet connection" is a warning that even native products often tie geofence evaluation to a server round-trip. An offline-first design should evaluate locally and timestamp locally; ServiceNow's "Geofence could not be validated" flag is the honest label for an offline check-in.
- Web push on iOS is conditional on install-to-Home-Screen and a user gesture. A customer who opens the TurnUp link once in Safari will get no push. The customer channel therefore needs an SMS or email fallback for every state change that matters (window breached, trade arrived), with push as an enhancement for installed users; the trade's dashboard, used daily, is a better candidate for install.

### Gaps
- Apple's current `CLCircularRegion` reference and `CLVisit` documentation are JavaScript-rendered and did not fetch; the figures above come from Apple's archived Location Awareness guide (last revised 2016) and are consistent with current behaviour reported on Apple's forums, but a current official minimum radius and the `CLVisit` arrival/departure accuracy statements could not be quoted.
- Housecall Pro's GPS help page returned 404; sample frequency details are from a search summary.
- No vendor publishes measured geofence accuracy in rural UK; the only quantitative statement is Google's "several hundred meters to several kilometers" without Wi-Fi.
- EU availability of Home Screen web push on iOS 17.4+ is reported by one vendor and not verified against Apple.

## Key question 4 — Detecting that the trade left and came back (materials run), and presenting inferred intervals

### Takeaway
Timesheet products detect a break in presence by a geofence exit followed by re-entry with a dwell/exit grace period (Timeero 1 minute, Jobber up to 3 minutes, ServiceM8 unspecified), then present the result as an editable suggestion rather than a fact; ServiceM8 goes further and lets the trade book the trip as paid travel time at check-out. A web app can only detect this if the page is alive, so TurnUp's "Paused" stage is the right manual primitive, with location captured at pause and resume.

### Cited Findings
- ServiceM8 check-out: "If you leave a job for a supplier or similar stop, you can add Trip time at check-out. This covers travel from your previous stop to the Trip destination, time spent there, and travel back to the job address"; the app "checks you out when you leave its location, even if you checked in manually" and rolls the recorded time back to the actual departure. — [ServiceM8: How Automated Time Tracking Works](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works)
- ServiceM8 on clock-off: "If you don't manually clock off and ServiceM8 lacked certainty, it suggests earlier clock-off times based on your movements when you clock off." — same source
- Timeero: exit grace of "approximately 1 minute"; "If you return within that window, you will remain clocked in." — [Timeero help](https://help.timeero.com/how-to-use-auto-clock-in/out-as-an-employee)
- Jobber: "After you leave the geofence, the time entry can take up to three minutes to update"; auto-started entries appear in Timesheets. — [Jobber help: location timers](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/location-timers-in-the-jobber-app/)
- Android's `DWELL` transition with a loitering delay is the platform primitive for ignoring brief exits/entries ("alert spam"). — [Android developers: geofencing](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing)
- Apple's `CLVisit` service reports arrivals and departures as a low-power alternative to continuous tracking (listed in Apple's background-mode table). — [Apple Core Location guide, Table 1-1](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/LocationAwarenessPG/CoreLocation/CoreLocation.html)
- Consumer precedent for "we think you were here, confirm?": Google Maps Timeline separates visited from "unconfirmed places" and "the accuracy of the places list is dependent on you confirming a visit instead of just relying on where Maps thinks you are." — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2020/01/09/google-maps-2019-timeline/)

### Inferences
- The industry presentation is consistent: show the inferred intervals, label them as inferred, and make confirmation one tap. A sensible TurnUp rendering is "On site 09:04-10:40, away 10:40-11:15 (materials?), on site 11:15-13:00. Looks right?" with the away segment defaulting to Paused and a reason picker (materials, another job, break). Timeero's 1-minute and Jobber's 3-minute grace values bracket the debounce needed so that walking to the van does not register as leaving.
- In a web app the leave/return can only be inferred from fixes the page actually took, which means from taps. The product should therefore make "Paused" cheap to tap (one gesture from the lock-screen-adjacent PWA icon) and capture a fix at Pause and at Resume; if the trade forgets, the fix taken at "Job done" plus the gap since the last fix allows a prompt ("No activity since 10:40 — were you away for part of this?").
- Customers should see the Paused stage with a reason ("Will has gone for a part, back by about 11:15") because an unexplained absence is what turns into a dispute; the DPD/Royal Mail pattern of always stating the next step applies.

### Gaps
- No vendor documents the actual UI copy of a "we think you were here from X to Y, is that right?" prompt; ServiceM8's update page has a screenshot I could not read. The wording above is my design inference, not a quoted pattern.
- `CLVisit` arrival/departure timing accuracy (Apple's reference) could not be fetched.

## Key question 5 — Overruns and the knock-on to the next customer

### Takeaway
Delivery and ride-hailing recompute ETAs continuously and show them (DPD's live 15-minute slot, Amazon's "stops away", Uber's ETA with a 5-minute lateness threshold); field-service vendors describe cascading the delay to later jobs and messaging affected customers once lateness crosses a 15-30 minute threshold, but only in marketing copy. For one van the simplest proven mechanism is an ordered day with a running "expected start" per job that shifts when the current job's end slips.

### Cited Findings
- DPD recalculates and narrows the window during the day, from a one-hour slot down to "a final 15 minute timeslot" on the live map; the customer can act on it (change date, neighbour, safe place, shop) "up to 15 minutes before final handover". — [DPD UK](https://www.dpd.co.uk/content/products_services/predict_fmp.jsp); [DPD CH](https://www.dpd.com/ch/en/predict)
- Amazon shows "the number of stops between the driver and your home", only when 10 or fewer stops remain. — [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2018/05/28/amazon-improves-package-tracking-capabilities/)
- Uber treats a driver more than 5 minutes past the initial ETA as a lateness event that changes the rider's rights (fee waived). — [Uber UK blog](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/changes-to-cancellation-fees)
- Uber's ETA serving constraint is milliseconds and the model is retrained on massive trip history; the sibling DeepETT system claims a 6% improvement in long-trip arrival accuracy. — [Uber Engineering: DeepETA](https://www.uber.com/blog/deepeta-how-uber-predicts-arrival-times/); [Uber Engineering: scaling real-time traffic](https://www.uber.com/blog/scaling-real-time-traffic/)
- Field-service vendor description (marketing, unverified): "When a job overruns, the system calculates the downstream impact and flags the affected jobs", a lateness threshold "typically 15-30 minutes", revised ETA "based on current crew location, remaining job duration, and travel time to the next appointment", notification by SMS/email with reply-to-reschedule. — [Fieldproxy](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/automations/window-crew-running-late-notify); [Fieldproxy on rerouting after overrun](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/articles/auto-reroute-50-field-agents-job-runs-over-budget)
- Energy suppliers may not rearrange with less than one working day's notice without the customer's recorded agreement (reg 3(7)); a same-day shift of a later job is therefore, in the regulated analogue, a conversation, not a notification. — [SI 2015/1544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1544/made)
- Housecall Pro's live-location link is deliberately time-limited: it expires at Start/Finish/Invoice or after 2 hours. — [Housecall Pro: Vehicle GPS tracking overview](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6060351-vehicle-gps-tracking-overview)

### Inferences
- For a one-van day, propagation is arithmetic: expected_start(next) = max(booked window start, projected end(current) + drive). Projected end is unknown until the trade says so, so the honest trigger is the trade's own input ("this will take another hour") or the window end passing without "Job done". The product should ask the trade for a revised finish when the booked duration is 75% elapsed, and only message the next customer when the recomputed start falls outside their window, mirroring the 15-30 minute vendor threshold and Uber's 5-minute idea scaled to trade windows.
- The message to the next customer should carry a new *window*, not a time, and the options DPD offers at that moment: keep it, move to later today, or rebook. Because the regulated norm treats same-day rearrangement as needing the customer's agreement, "we've moved you" is the wrong framing; "would you rather wait or rebook" is the right one.
- Live location to the next customer should start only once the trade is actually "On my way" and should expire on arrival or after a bounded period (Housecall Pro's 2-hour expiry is the precedent), both for privacy and because a stale map is worse than none.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of how accurately any field-service product predicts job overrun; the "80-90% within 15 minutes" claims seen in search are vendor claims and were not included.
- No published UK data on what proportion of trade jobs overrun their booked duration.

## Key question 6 — The customer side: "I'm not home / running late", rescheduling from a link, handing over to someone else

### Takeaway
The proven customer-side controls are: reply-to-cancel/reschedule on the reminder (NHS two-way SMS, cutting DNAs 20-30%), in-flight redirect to a neighbour or safe place up to minutes before arrival (DPD), online rebooking with a notice period (Royal Mail 48 working hours; BT two working days; trade guidance 24 hours), and an explicit "an adult 18+ must be present for the whole window" rule that doubles as the handover mechanism (Openreach).

### Cited Findings
- NHS two-way reminders: "The patient can then reply with requests to either cancel or reschedule appointments"; "reply 1 to confirm, 2 to reschedule, 3 to cancel"; cancelled slots can be reallocated. — [Digital Health: Barts](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2012/01/barts-cuts-dnas-with-text-service/); [BT healthcare use case](https://business.bt.com/content/dam/bt-business/pdfs/corporate/products/enabling-your-people/bt-2-way-messaging-healthcare-use-case.pdf)
- DPD: redirect "up to 15 minutes before final handover" to another date, a neighbour, a safe place, or a pickup shop. — [DPD CH](https://www.dpd.com/ch/en/predict)
- Royal Mail: redelivery to "another address in your postcode area" or a shop/locker, booked online with 48 working hours' notice; automatic retry next working day. — [Royal Mail redelivery](https://www.royalmail.com/personal/receiving-mail/redelivery); [Royal Mail auto-redelivery](https://www.royalmail.com/auto-redelivery)
- Openreach: "someone aged 18 or over is at home for the full appointment window"; otherwise rebook through the provider. — [Openreach](https://www.openreach.com/help-and-support/what-will-happen-during-my-repair-visit); ISPs restate "a representative aged over 18 must stay for the whole slot". — [XLN](https://help.xln.co.uk/en/articles/10031980-broadband-installation)
- BT terms: "if you need to change or cancel an appointment, you must tell us at least two working days beforehand." — [ISPreview, Nov 2025](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/11/broadband-isp-bt-ponders-possible-50-charge-for-missed-uk-appointments.html)
- Uber: London UberX no longer auto-cancels after 8 minutes of rider no-show; the driver chooses to wait or cancel. — [Uber UK blog](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/changes-to-wait-times)
- Trade guidance: day-before confirmation and follow-up call if no reply; no-access fee trigger "if we cannot gain access at the agreed time and you have not given us at least 24 hours' notice". — [Trade2base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-no-access-uk)
- Consumer-law backdrop: the customer's statutory 14-day cancellation right for off-premises/distance bookings, with the urgent-repairs carve-out and the need for an express request before work starts in the period. — [SI 2013/3134 reg 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28/made); [reg 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36/made)

### Inferences
- The customer's TurnUp link should carry, at every stage before "On site", three buttons that map directly to the proven patterns: "I'm running late — give me X minutes" (Uber's rider-side wait), "Someone else will let you in" (Openreach's 18+ representative rule; capture a name and number), and "Rebook" (NHS reply-2 / Royal Mail online rebooking). The rebook route should state the notice rule and the consequence plainly, because that is what makes a later no-access fee fair (KQ2).
- The day-before reminder is the highest-leverage moment (the NHS evidence is strongest there), and the one where a one-word reply or one-tap confirm costs the customer nothing. Reply options should include a "someone else will be there" path so the handover is recorded before the day.
- Access-refused-at-door and wrong-address cases are best handled by the same "sorry we missed you" record plus an immediate choice, not by a fee by default; the carriers' practice is to offer the next step first.

### Gaps
- I found no UK study measuring how often tradespeople's customers self-report lateness or hand over to a third party; the mechanisms above are borrowed from health, parcels and telecoms.

## Key question 7 — Evidence and disputes: what resolves "you never came"

### Takeaway
What settles a non-attendance dispute in practice is a chain of independently timestamped events (device fix, server receipt, outbound messages with delivery receipts, a door photo whose composition identifies the property), kept in original form; carriers rely on exactly scan + geolocation + photo, and the civil standard is the balance of probabilities. Metadata helps but is fragile, so the server-side record matters more than EXIF.

### Cited Findings
- Carrier claims practice: a claim "will be rejected if tracking shows delivery with an End of Life (EOL) scan, GEO location, and photo evidence." — [Shippo: Evri claims](https://support.goshippo.com/hc/en-us/articles/7711950911259-Submitting-Shipping-Claims-For-Evri-Shipments)
- Photos that do not identify the property are disputed and undermine trust (Evri photos "that didn't match their delivery location"); consumer guidance: "a photograph does not automatically mean a successful delivery". — [Resolver](https://news.resolver.co.uk/resolver-stories-evri-courier-chaos/); [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/news/what-are-your-consumer-rights-for-late-or-missing-parcels/amp)
- DPD keeps delivery photos for 3 months and lets the recipient refuse a photo in favour of a signature. — [DPD BE FAQ](https://www.dpd.com/be/en/contact/faq/receiving-parcels/?post=21)
- Photo metadata has decided litigation: in a High Court shipping case "The time details contained in the photo metadata requested contradicted many witness statements." — [KordaMentha: Loose metadata sinks ships](https://www.kordamentha.com/insights/loose-metadata-sinks-ships)
- Timestamps are used "to determine whether a photo was taken at the time of an incident or taken at a different time to fabricate evidence associated with that incident." — [JD Supra: mobile device metadata](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/going-mobile-metadata-considerations-8621144)
- Field-service products keep a movement log for exactly this purpose: ServiceM8 points businesses to "the Job Checkout Report to review recorded movements and on-site time" when accountability is the goal. — [ServiceM8 help Q&A](https://support.servicem8.com/questions/staff/can-servicem8-restrict-technicians-to-checking-into-jobs-only-when-they-are-on-site)
- Automated compensation schemes avoid disputes entirely by defining the trigger objectively (engineer "cancels or misses an appointment with less than 24 hours' notice") and paying without a claim. — [broadband.co.uk guide](https://www.broadband.co.uk/broadband/help/guide-automatic-broadband-landline-compensation)
- Trade guidance already frames the confirmation message as evidence: "send a confirmation message so there's a timestamped record." — [Trade2base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-no-access-uk)

### Inferences
- The most defensible evidence set for a trade is the one the customer helped create: a booking confirmation they received, a day-before reminder they confirmed, an "On my way" they opened (server can log link opens), an "On site" event with a device fix and server time, a front-door photo, the text/call attempts with timestamps, and a "Job done" with before/after photos and (optionally) a signature. Each item alone is weak; the sequence is strong because the customer's own device generated some of it.
- Store photos with server receipt time and the fix taken at capture, and burn a visible stamp into the image for the customer-facing copy while keeping the original. EXIF alone is strippable; the server-side record is what a small-claims judge or an ombudsman will find persuasive on the balance of probabilities.
- Compose door photos so the property is identifiable (number, door, street context) and never the interior; the Evri disputes are entirely about unidentifiable photos.

### Gaps
- I found no UK small-claims or ombudsman decision specifically turning on a tradesperson's GPS or photo evidence of attendance; the inference is from carrier claims practice and general evidence law.
- No source quantifies how often "you never came" disputes arise for UK trades.

## Key question 8 — Taxonomy of eventualities and sensible defaults

### Takeaway
Every eventuality is a combination of three variables (trade present/late/absent, customer present/late/absent/represented, channel working/offline/dead) plus a few singular events (wrong address, access refused, cancelled en route). The defaults below are each anchored to a mechanism documented above.

### Inferences
- Three design rules fall out of the table. (1) Every state change the customer cares about has a non-push fallback. (2) Every automatic inference (arrival, departure, lateness) is shown as "we think" with one-tap correction, never silently written. (3) Any fee is symmetrical, pre-agreed, avoidable with notice, and proportionate, or it is not charged.
- The "Paused" stage already in TurnUp is the differentiator most products lack on the customer side: carriers and utilities never explain a gap; the NHS cost-messaging trial suggests specific, concrete explanations change behaviour, and a stated reason plus return time is the honest equivalent.

### Gaps
- Default wait duration at the door (I suggest ~10 minutes with two contact attempts) is not sourced to any UK standard; carriers wait none and the trade guidance only says not to let the next job slip.
- No source gives a measured customer tolerance for lateness messages (how many minutes late before a message is wanted); the window-end trigger is inferred from the regulated schemes.
