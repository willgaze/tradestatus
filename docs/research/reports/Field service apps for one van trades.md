# Make the bad day TurnUp's best feature

*Research report, 10 October 2026. Synthesised from four research notes (failure modes and UK law; ServiceM8; UK/ANZ apps; North American apps) researched the same day, with an earlier note on formal staged processes as background. Every figure below carries the source it came from; where the researchers could not reach a primary page they said so, and this report repeats the caveat rather than hiding it.*

Every field-service app a one-van trade can afford treats the visit as a single "on my way" text and then goes silent, and the only products that give the customer a live map gate it behind vehicle-tracker hardware, four-licence minimums or Enterprise portals. None of the sixteen UK, ANZ and North American apps surveyed has a customer-visible "customer not home", "paused" or "running late" state, none automates a lateness message, and none productises the evidence that settles a "you never came" dispute. Meanwhile the benchmark UK customers carry into a plumber's visit comes from regulated industries: an arrival *window* rather than a time, a 24-hour notice rule that applies to both sides, and an automatic fixed payment when the trader misses — **£32.31** under Ofcom's scheme and **£30** under the energy Guaranteed Standards — paid without the customer having to ask ([broadband.co.uk](https://www.broadband.co.uk/broadband/help/guide-automatic-broadband-landline-compensation); [SI 2015/1544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1544/made)). TurnUp's structural advantage is that its page persists through On site, Paused and Job done, which is exactly where every competitor's link expires; its structural limit is that a web app cannot wake on arrival, read location with the screen off, or push to an iPhone that has not installed it, so proof of attendance must be built from tap-time evidence with three timestamps, and ServiceM8's "looks like you arrived at 09:04" can only be approximated, not copied, until there is a native shell. ServiceM8 is the right first connector — its automated check-in, roll-back and Trip-time logic are the best-designed in the market — but its customer surface ends at arrival, its webhooks carry no values, and it has no exception vocabulary at all, which leaves a server-side product plenty of room to be the layer that handles the bad day. The prioritised list at the end sorts roughly forty features into Copy, Improve, Prepare for and Do not do, so the job-management tool grows from the tracking link's own event log rather than being bolted on later.

## Customers arrive with a regulated benchmark: a window, 24 hours, and £30

The strongest consumer expectation a plumber meets at the door was not set by plumbers. Ofcom's Automatic Compensation scheme pays **£32.31** "if your provider's designated engineer cancels or misses an appointment with less than 24 hours' notice", **£10.34 per day** for a delayed repair after two working days and **£6.46 per day** for a delayed new service, all indexed annually and credited "usually within 30 days" by BT, EE, Sky, TalkTalk, Virgin Media, Vodafone and the rest ([broadband.co.uk guide](https://www.broadband.co.uk/broadband/help/guide-automatic-broadband-landline-compensation)). The researchers could not fetch Ofcom's own page (HTTP 403) and relied on a reseller guide checked September 2026 plus search summaries, which gave the same figure; the trajectory from **£26.24 in 2022** to **£31.19 in late 2025** shows the annual uplift ([ISPreview, 2022](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2022/06/25-missed-appointment-fee-angers-virgin-media-uk-customers.html); [ISPreview, Nov 2025](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/11/broadband-isp-bt-ponders-possible-50-charge-for-missed-uk-appointments.html)). Energy is stricter and statutory: the Electricity and Gas (Standards of Performance) (Suppliers) Regulations 2015 require an appointment slot "no more than 4 hours long", forbid rearranging "less than 1 working day prior" without the customer's recorded agreement, and trigger "a payment of £30" within ten working days, with a further £30 if that payment is itself late ([SI 2015/1544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1544/made)). British Gas paid **£1.1 million** for missing appointments and then missing the ten-day payment deadline ([Ofgem](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/publications/british-gas-pays-ps11m-compensate-customers-after-agents-missed-appointments)). Openreach's own customer guidance completes the picture: the engineer will "arrive within the confirmed time slot", "someone aged 18 or over" must be home "for the full appointment window", and if nobody is, "charges may apply for missed appointments" ([Openreach](https://www.openreach.com/help-and-support/what-will-happen-during-my-repair-visit)).

The shape of that benchmark matters more than the money. It is a bounded window, a day-before reminder, a symmetrical notice rule, and a consequence that fires automatically. No regulated operator escalates to a phone call; the documented ladder is reminder, on-the-day message, state change plus credit. Customers mostly do not chase — Citizens Advice found **55%** of people with a broadband problem never sought redress and the rest "had to repeatedly negotiate" ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/about-us/media-centre/press-releases/citizens-advice-responds-to-ofcom-consultation/)) — which is precisely why the regulated schemes make the payment automatic, and why TurnUp's defaults should fire without being asked. A one-van business cannot copy the £30, but it can copy the structure: a window the customer can see, a visible "window passed" state, and a pre-agreed gesture (an apology, a discount, priority rebooking) that the trade configures once and the system sends.

Tradespeople are measured against this benchmark and currently fail it on communication rather than competence. Markel's April 2026 survey of 500 homeowners and 500 tradespeople found **35%** of homeowners call failing to turn up a "major inconvenience", **25%** cite short-notice cancellation and **19%** cite lateness, with regional no-show experience ranging from **10%** in the North West to **31%** in Yorkshire; the same survey found tradespeople's second-biggest wish, at **45%**, is "the homeowner being home and on time" ([Markel](https://www.markeluk.com/knowledge-centre/tradie-etiquette-uk)). HomeServe found **36%** of Britons said their last tradesperson was late and one in a hundred never came ([HomeServe via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/waiting-in-drain-homeserve-discover-brits-spend-72m-hours-a-year-waiting-for-plumbers-201574671.html)). The no-show problem is symmetrical — a leaked BT email claimed "about 10% of fibre installs are missed on the day by customers, despite reminders" and floated a £50 customer no-show charge ([ISPreview, Nov 2025](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/11/broadband-isp-bt-ponders-possible-50-charge-for-missed-uk-appointments.html)) — so the product should treat both directions with the same machinery. The customer's "I'm running late myself" button is worth as much to the trade as the trade's "On my way" is to the customer.

Two statutes set the limits on what a trade can charge when the customer is the one who fails. The Consumer Rights Act 2015 grey list treats as potentially unfair any term letting the trader keep sums when the consumer cancels without an equivalent payment "where the trader is the party cancelling", any term requiring a consumer "to pay a disproportionately high sum", and any term letting the trader "alter the terms unilaterally without a valid reason" ([CRA 2015 Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/schedule/2)). GOV.UK's consumer guide says a cancellation charge "is not fair just because it's in the contract you signed", must be "a genuine estimate of the business' direct loss", and that "businesses must take reasonable steps to reduce their losses" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cancelling-goods-or-services-guide-for-consumers/cancelling-goods-or-services)); the CMA adds that an unfair term "can't be enforced against the customer even if they have signed up to it" ([CMA](https://competitionandmarkets.blog.gov.uk/2019/06/27/holiday-deposits-and-cancellation-fees-are-you-being-treated-fairly/)). UK trade guidance translates that into practice: a no-access fee "is only enforceable if the customer agreed to it before the job", typical amounts are **£30–£60** for local domestic work, the trigger wording is "if we cannot gain access at the agreed time and you have not given us at least 24 hours' notice", and the advice is to "leave a calling card and send a confirmation message so there's a timestamped record" ([Trade2base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-no-access-uk)). Virgin Media's **£25** no-answer charge, forewarned at booking, is the utility precedent — and readers attacked the asymmetry when Virgin's own miss paid only marginally more ([ISPreview, 2022](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2022/06/25-missed-appointment-fee-angers-virgin-media-uk-customers.html)). A fee survives only if it is shown before booking, mirrored, avoidable with notice and proportionate; a flat fee with no notice route is paragraph 6 of the grey list.

The Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 govern the other direction. A booking made by phone or online carries a **14-day** cancellation right, extended by up to twelve months if the trader fails to give the cancellation information, with refunds due within 14 days ([SI 2013/3134 Part 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/part/3)). Regulation 28(1)(e) exempts "urgent repairs or maintenance" the consumer specifically requested — but only the urgent work itself; extra services or non-essential parts supplied on the same visit remain cancellable ([reg 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28/made)). Regulation 36 is the one that bites a plumber who starts work within the fortnight: the trader "must not begin the supply of a service before the end of the cancellation period" without an express request, and if the cancellation information was never given, "the consumer bears no cost for supply of the service, in full or in part" ([reg 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36/made)). Citizens Advice's plain-English version puts the off-premises threshold at **£42** and starts the clock "the day after you enter a contract" ([Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/cancelling-a-service-youve-arranged/)). The practical consequence for TurnUp is that the booking confirmation — the first thing the Booked in stage sends — is the durable medium that must carry, in order, the cancellation information, an express "please start before the 14 days are up" acknowledgement, and the no-access and short-notice terms. Get the first wrong and the right to charge for the whole visit evaporates. None of the surveyed apps builds this into its confirmation; the researchers found no vendor documenting cancellation fees, deposits or late-cancellation rules inside the product at all.

## Seventeen eventualities reduce to three design rules

Every bad day is a combination of three variables — the trade present, late or absent; the customer present, late, absent or represented; the channel working, offline or dead — plus a handful of singular events: wrong address, access refused, cancelled en route. The table below gives each a proactive default and names the mechanism it borrows. The defaults were derived by the failure-modes researcher from the sources in this report; where a number is a design judgement rather than a documented standard (the ten-minute door wait, the 75% overrun prompt) it is labelled as such.

| # | Eventuality | Proactive default | Borrowed from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both present, on time | "On site" tap captures a location fix and three timestamps; customer page moves stage; any live map stops at arrival | Housecall Pro link expiry at Start ([HCP](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6060351-vehicle-gps-tracking-overview)) |
| 2 | Trade drifting late inside the window | Update the page silently; no message until the window end approaches | DPD narrowing a one-hour slot to 15 minutes ([DPD](https://www.dpd.co.uk/content/products_services/predict_fmp.jsp)); Uber's 5-minute ETA threshold ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/changes-to-cancellation-fees)) |
| 3 | Window end passes with no "On site" | Auto-message the customer with a new *window* and three options (wait, later today, rebook); dashboard nags the trade; pre-agreed goodwill gesture fires | Ofcom/Ofgem automatic payment on an objective trigger ([broadband.co.uk](https://www.broadband.co.uk/broadband/help/guide-automatic-broadband-landline-compensation); [SI 2015/1544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1544/made)) |
| 4 | Trade cancels before the day | Dashboard cancel records the notice time; customer gets a rebook link; 24-hour rule applies to the trade too | Ofcom 24 hours; energy one working day (same) |
| 5 | Trade cancels en route or same day | Treated as a missed appointment: apology, goodwill, priority rebook; reason logged | Reg 3(7) ([SI 2015/1544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1544/made)) |
| 6 | Customer says "running late myself" | Accept a stated delay up to a cap; shift the trade's expected start; propagate to later jobs | Uber's rider-wait rules ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/blog/changes-to-wait-times)) |
| 7 | Customer not home | Short fixed wait (c. 10 minutes, a design judgement), two logged contact attempts, door photo plus fix plus timestamps, "sorry we missed you" message with rebook link; fee only if pre-agreed and no notice given | Trade2base, Royal Mail card-and-retry, CRA 2015 ([Trade2base](https://www.trade2base.com/blog/trade-business-no-access-uk); [Royal Mail](https://www.royalmail.com/auto-redelivery); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cancelling-goods-or-services-guide-for-consumers/cancelling-goods-or-services)) |
| 8 | Someone else lets the trade in | Allowed if an adult; capture name and number before the day where possible; same evidence trail | Openreach's 18+ rule ([Openreach](https://www.openreach.com/help-and-support/what-will-happen-during-my-repair-visit)) |
| 9 | Access refused, unsafe, cannot proceed | Reason plus photo; visit marked attended-not-completed; rebook offered; no fee by default | CRA Sch 2 para 6 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/schedule/2)) |
| 10 | Wrong address | Trade taps "wrong address" with a fix; customer asked to confirm or pin the location; nobody's no-show unless the customer supplied it | ServiceM8's dependence on an address picked from the dropdown ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works)) |
| 11 | No signal | Taps still accepted; device time and last fix stamped; event flagged "location not validated"; queued and synced on reconnect; never dropped | ServiceNow's offline flag; Navixy's drop-after-failure as the anti-pattern ([ServiceNow](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/IVKDA~MgX_6UMb0D9l5eLg/Q3s_ccTVKLFaJ~~yAOmK1A); [Navixy](https://navixy.com/docs/user/guide/x-gps-mobile-apps/x-gps-tracker/offline-mode)) |
| 12 | Trade's phone dead | Dashboard shows "no update since hh:mm"; customer page shows the last stage honestly with its time; trade back-fills later under a "reconstructed" label | ServiceM8 wind-back ([ServiceM8](https://www.servicem8.com/updates/check-in-time-adjustment)) |
| 13 | Customer's push not delivered | Every customer-critical event also goes by SMS or email; push is an enhancement | WebKit's Home Screen requirement ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)) |
| 14 | Materials run | "Paused" with a reason and expected return; fix at pause and resume; gaps inferred from fixes shown as suggestions to confirm | ServiceM8 Trip time; Timeero and Jobber grace periods ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works); [Timeero](https://help.timeero.com/how-to-use-auto-clock-in/out-as-an-employee)) |
| 15 | Job overruns | Ask the trade for a revised finish when the booked duration is c. 75% elapsed (design judgement); recompute the next job's start; message that customer only if it leaves their window, with options | Vendor 15–30 minute threshold, marketing only ([Fieldproxy](https://www.fieldproxy.ai/automations/window-crew-running-late-notify)) |
| 16 | Customer cancels in cooling-off | Honour 14 days unless the visit was urgent repair specifically requested; charge proportionately only if an express start request was recorded | Regs 28 and 36 ([reg 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28/made); [reg 36](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/36/made)) |
| 17 | "You never came" | Produce the event chain: confirmation, reminder reply, link opens, fix and server times, door photo, message attempts | Carrier claims practice ([Shippo](https://support.goshippo.com/hc/en-us/articles/7711950911259-Submitting-Shipping-Claims-For-Evri-Shipments)) |

Three rules fall out of that table. **Every state change the customer cares about has a non-push fallback**, because a customer who opened the link once in Safari will never receive a push. **Every automatic inference is shown as "we think" with one-tap correction**, never silently written, because that is what ServiceM8, Jobber and Timeero all do and what the Google Maps Timeline precedent established: "the accuracy of the places list is dependent on you confirming a visit instead of just relying on where Maps thinks you are" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2020/01/09/google-maps-2019-timeline/)). **Any fee is symmetrical, pre-agreed, avoidable with notice and proportionate, or it is not charged.**

Two of the eventualities deserve more than a row. The lateness message should be concrete, because concreteness is the one thing the evidence says changes behaviour: in a randomised trial of 10,111 NHS patients, a reminder stating the exact cost of a missed appointment produced an **8.4%** no-show rate against **11.1%** for the generic message, and "mentioning costs in general terms was significantly less effective than stating the exact amount" ([Warwick Business School](https://www.wbs.ac.uk/news/how-to-stop-people-missing-hospital-appointments/); [PMC4569397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569397)). "Will is 25 minutes behind; new arrival 10:25–10:45" beats "running late". The message should also carry a window and a choice rather than a fiat, because the regulated analogue — energy's reg 3(7) — treats same-day rearrangement as needing the customer's agreement: "would you rather wait or rebook" is the right frame, "we've moved you" is the wrong one. And the day-before reminder is the highest-leverage moment of the whole sequence: two-way SMS where the patient replies to confirm, reschedule or cancel cut missed appointments by "a phenomenal 30 per cent" at Brighton and "more than 20%" in some Barts services ([Brighton & Hove News](https://brightonandhovenews.org/?p=52854); [Digital Health](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2012/01/barts-cuts-dnas-with-text-service/)). The reply options should include "someone else will be there", so the handover in row 8 is recorded before the day rather than discovered on the doorstep.

The customer-not-home record is where the carriers have done the design work. DPD drivers take a photo as proof, recipients can refuse the photo and sign instead, and photos are kept for three months ([DPD BE FAQ](https://www.dpd.com/be/en/contact/faq/receiving-parcels/?post=21)); Royal Mail leaves a card, retries automatically the next working day and lets the customer rebook online with 48 working hours' notice ([Royal Mail](https://www.royalmail.com/auto-redelivery)); Amazon's photo "will focus on the placement of the package" ([EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2018/05/28/amazon-improves-package-tracking-capabilities/)). The failure mode is equally well documented: Evri photos "that didn't match their delivery location" generate disputes, and consumer guidance warns "a photograph does not automatically mean a successful delivery" ([Resolver](https://news.resolver.co.uk/resolver-stories-evri-courier-chaos/); [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/news/what-are-your-consumer-rights-for-late-or-missing-parcels/amp)). A carrier's claims desk rejects a loss claim when "tracking shows delivery with an End of Life scan, GEO location, and photo evidence" ([Shippo](https://support.goshippo.com/hc/en-us/articles/7711950911259-Submitting-Shipping-Claims-For-Evri-Shipments)), and photo metadata has decided litigation when "the time details contained in the photo metadata contradicted many witness statements" ([KordaMentha](https://www.kordamentha.com/insights/loose-metadata-sinks-ships)). So the TurnUp "sorry we missed you" record is: arrival fix and timestamps, a front-door photo composed so the house is identifiable (number, door, street context, never the interior), the attempts made with their times, how long the trade waited, and a rebook link — with the originals stored server-side under receipt time, because EXIF is strippable and the server record is what persuades on the balance of probabilities. The strongest version of that chain is the one the customer helped create: they received the confirmation, replied to the reminder, and opened the "On my way" link, all of which the server can log.

## A browser cannot wake on arrival, so proof of attendance is tap-time evidence

The question "can TurnUp do GPS arrival detection the way ServiceM8 does it" has a precise answer: not as a web app, and only partially as a native one. ServiceM8 checks a technician in when they "arrive at the location of a Job that has been allocated to you", checks them out "when you leave its location", and because "automatic check-in/out isn't instant", it rolls "the recorded time of that event back to the time you actually arrived or left"; if it lacked confidence, tapping Start job "lets you wind the check-in time back to when it detected your arrival", every automatic action can be undone by tapping its push notification, and it requires "Always Allow" with "Precise Location" ([ServiceM8: How Automated Time Tracking Works](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works); [ServiceM8 update](https://www.servicem8.com/updates/check-in-time-adjustment)). That depends on operating-system machinery a browser is never given.

The native primitives are well specified. On iOS, "regions associated with your app are tracked at all times, including when the app isn't running"; a boundary crossing relaunches the app in the background with "around 10 seconds" to act; the system debounces by requiring the device to cross the boundary, move a minimum distance away and stay there "for at least 20 seconds"; and "if Wi-Fi is disabled, region monitoring is significantly less accurate", with a working minimum distance of "approximately 200 meters" ([Apple Location Awareness guide, archived](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/LocationAwarenessPG/RegionMonitoring/RegionMonitoring.html)). Android's Geofencing API allows **100** geofences per app, recommends a radius of **100–150 m** because "when Wi-Fi location isn't available (for example, when you are driving in rural areas) the location accuracy degrades" to "several hundred meters to several kilometers", offers a `DWELL` transition with a loitering delay to suppress "alert spam", quotes latency of "less than 2 minutes" moving and "up to 6 minutes" stationary, warns that "if Wi-Fi is turned off, your application might never get geofence alerts", and requires re-registration after every reboot ([Android developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing)). The researchers note that Apple's current API reference is JavaScript-rendered and did not fetch, so the iOS figures come from the archived guide, consistent with current forum reports but not re-quoted from a 2026 page.

Even native products struggle with this. Jobber's location timers "trigger within 200 meters" of the geofence, can take "up to three minutes to update" after leaving, and — the admission that matters most for rural Wiltshire — "require an active internet connection to detect when you enter or exit a location", so Jobber itself recommends manual tracking in low-connectivity areas ([Jobber Help](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/location-timers-in-the-jobber-app/)); a community thread records auto timers that "worked for about a couple of weeks and now have not worked for 2 months" ([Jobber Community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/job-details-scheduling/location-timers-still-not-working/8186)). ServiceTitan's Arrive by GPS marks a technician arrived within **125 metres**, but only if the office has validated the address, and quotes cellular-fallback accuracy of around 500 m ([ServiceTitan Help](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/arrive-by-gps-for-servicetitan-mobile)). Timeero's auto clock-in waits two minutes inside the fence but records "the actual time you entered the geofence", with a one-minute exit grace ([Timeero](https://help.timeero.com/how-to-use-auto-clock-in/out-as-an-employee)). The industry pattern is therefore consistent and worth stating plainly: the *detected* time is the record, the tap is confirmation, and the user interface always shows "we think" with an undo.

What a web app gets instead is a much shorter list, and the matrix below is the honest statement of it.

| Capability | Native app | Web app / PWA | Consequence for TurnUp today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wake on arrival at a job address | Yes: iOS region monitoring, Android geofence `DWELL` | No; a WebKit request for service-worker background geolocation was rejected ([WebKit bug 193946](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=193946)) | No automatic "On site"; arrival is a tap |
| Location with the screen off | Yes with "Always" permission | No: `watchPosition` "may stop reporting the user's location when the screen is turned off" ([Progressier](https://progressier.com/pwa-capabilities/geolocation)) | No background trail; fixes exist only at taps or while the page is open on a dash mount |
| Background upload of queued events | Yes | Only on Chromium; Background Sync "is not Baseline because it does not work in some of the most widely-used browsers" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Background_Synchronization_API)) | Queue in IndexedDB; retry on `online` and visibility events; on iOS the retry happens when the PWA is next foregrounded |
| Push to the trade | Yes | Yes if installed; on iOS only for Home Screen web apps since 16.4, and only after a user gesture ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)) | Install the dashboard to the Home Screen as part of onboarding |
| Push to the customer | Yes | Same iOS constraint; one vendor reports Home Screen push unavailable in EU countries on iOS 17.4+, unverified against Apple ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)) | Assume no customer push; SMS or email for every critical event |
| Send SMS from the product | Yes via gateway, same as web | Yes via gateway; a native app adds nothing here | Capability gap is a gateway and a sender ID, not an app |
| Take payments | Yes, including Tap to Pay | Yes for card links and open banking; Tap to Pay on the phone is native only | Deposits and pay-links are web-feasible; on-the-doorstep tap is not |
| Camera and current position at a tap | Yes | Yes: `getCurrentPosition` and file input work in the foreground | Door photos and tap-time fixes are fully available now |

Given that matrix, the achievable proof of attendance for a web TurnUp is tap-time evidence done properly. When the trade taps "On site", capture a high-accuracy `getCurrentPosition` fix, the device clock and the server receipt time, and store all three. The gap between the first two and the third is the offline interval and is itself evidence rather than a defect; ServiceNow's label for a check-in made without connectivity — "Geofence could not be validated" — is the honest flag to carry ([ServiceNow docs](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/IVKDA~MgX_6UMb0D9l5eLg/Q3s_ccTVKLFaJ~~yAOmK1A)). Queue the event locally and never drop it: Navixy's documented behaviour, where "files that fail to transfer after reconnection are deleted from the queue and never sent", is the anti-pattern ([Navixy](https://navixy.com/docs/user/guide/x-gps-mobile-apps/x-gps-tracker/offline-mode)). ServiceM8 itself only syncs offline changes once back in range and cannot send SMS or generate PDFs offline ([ServiceM8 FAQ](https://support.servicem8.com/questions/mobile/does-servicem8-work-offline-without-mobile-coverage-or-internet)), so in a Pewsey Vale dead zone its Track My Arrival link simply never gets sent, because the link is only added "if ServiceM8 has the GPS location of your mobile device when the On-the-Way text is sent" ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/servicem8-add-ons/what-is-track-my-arrival)). A server-side customer page that says "last updated 09:04" and keeps showing the last honest stage is more robust than that, and TurnUp can advertise it.

The "looks like you arrived at 09:04, start from then?" prompt can be approximated as a *prompted reconstruction*. If the page took a fix earlier — at the "On my way" tap, or because the trade left the tab open on a dash mount — then a late "On site" tap can offer "You have been near this address since about 09:04 (from your last fix). Use 09:04?" The same logic handles the forgotten pause: the fix taken at "Job done" plus the gap since the last fix allows "No activity since 10:40 — were you away for part of this?" with a reason picker. It is strictly weaker than ServiceM8's version, because it only works when an earlier fix exists, and it should say so in the interface. Spotting a materials run without a background trail is likewise manual by construction: make "Paused" one gesture from the installed icon, capture a fix at Pause and at Resume, and tell the customer the reason — "Will has gone for a part, back by about 11:15" — because an unexplained absence is what turns into a dispute. ServiceM8's own model is instructive here: leaving the address checks you *out* rather than pausing, and the gap becomes an optional billed "Trip" at the next check-out ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works)); a single-visit timeline reading "on site 09:04–10:12, away 10:12–10:41 (materials), back 10:41–12:30 — looks right?" is a clearer presentation than any vendor documents, and the researchers found no vendor publishing the UI copy of such a prompt. When a native shell arrives, the right primitives are region monitoring on a 150–200 m radius around the day's addresses, Android `DWELL` with a one-to-two-minute loitering delay, "Always" permission on both platforms, local evaluation and timestamping rather than a server round-trip, and a budget of two to six minutes of latency with expected rural failure — and the geofence stays a suggestion with a visible "we think" and a one-tap correction.

## ServiceM8 owns arrival detection and nothing after it

ServiceM8 is job-centric: quotes, bookings, check-ins, photos, forms, messages and payments all hang off a Job card with a Diary, most capabilities are add-ons gated by plan tier rather than priced separately, and the 2025–2026 releases pushed into AI (Office Agent, Phone Agent, Auto-Quote, an MCP server for ChatGPT and Claude) and finally delivered a full Android app ([September 2026 update](https://www.servicem8.com/september-2026-update)). UK pricing is by job volume, not users: Free **£0** for one user and 30 jobs, Starter **£25** for 50 jobs and 100+ SMS, Growing **£59** for 150 jobs and 300+, Premium **£119**, Premium Plus **£269**, all ex VAT with extra SMS at **5p** and a 14-day trial without a card ([ServiceM8 UK pricing](https://www.servicem8.com/uk/pricing)). Forms, Assets, Proposals and the shared Inbox need Growing, so a one-van plumber at 40–60 jobs a month realistically pays £59 plus ServiceM8 Phone from £15 plus card fees of **1.65% + 20p** domestic or **0.75% + 20p** by open banking ([ServiceM8 Pay fees](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/10116208886927-ServiceM8-Pay-card-processing-fees-payout-speed); [PayByBank](http://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/servicem8-pay/how-to-use-paybybank-with-servicem8-pay-uk)). SMS at 5p with 100 included means TurnUp cannot compete on message cost; it has to compete on what the message contains and what it leads to.

**What ServiceM8 requires.** Automated time tracking needs iOS or the new Android app, Location "Always Allow" with Precise Location, and an address that was "selected from the dropdown"; detection only fires for a job "allocated to you, scheduled to you for the current day, or reached via the Navigate function", so a job parked in a queue or booked for another day will not auto check-in ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works)). The On-the-Way text requires tapping Start Job, enabling "Notify Client" (a mobile number must exist) and tapping Navigate; nothing is sent automatically when the technician leaves the previous job ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/servicem8-add-ons/what-is-track-my-arrival)). Booking Confirmation fires only for bookings made in the app or the job card's New Booking window — "dragging jobs onto the Schedule or Calendar will not send a booking confirmation" — and Booking Reminders go only to jobs carrying the reminder badge, by SMS only between 07:00 and 19:00 ([Booking Confirmation](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360002070355-Booking-Confirmation-Automation); [Booking Reminders](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/automation/how-to-set-automatic-booking-reminders)). A rescheduled customer may therefore never be told unless the trade rebooks through the right window or texts by hand; the researchers found no official statement on whether a reschedule re-triggers confirmation.

**What it does well.** The check-in logic is the best in the market and the thing to study rather than copy blind: detected arrival back-dated, a wind-back offer on Start job, suggested clock-off times "based on your movements", an undo on every automatic action by tapping its notification, and Trip time at check-out covering "travel from your previous stop to the Trip destination, time spent there, and travel back" ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001167415-How-Automated-Time-Tracking-Works)). The customer-facing journey is thin but coherent: a confirmation, a reminder, an On-the-Way SMS whose default template greets the contact by first name, names the staff member, says they will be "arriving in approximately x minutes", and asks the customer to call the technician's mobile if they cannot be there ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/200029719-Can-I-adjust-the-SMS-template-sent-to-clients-when-navigating-to-the-job)). Job Allocations replace a fixed time with a Booking Window, estimated work time and a Booking Expiry after which the job is un-allocated for reassignment ([Job Allocations](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/200272634-Job-Allocations-Overview-and-Setup)), and the account-level Arrival Window defaults to two hours when online booking is enabled ([Online Booking](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005042263-How-to-create-Online-Booking-Forms-Services)). The five pre-built automations — confirmation, reminder, feedback, payment follow-up, quote follow-up — are what reviewers praise ("Populate it once and start using it forever") ([Trustpilot UK](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/servicem8.com)). CarPlay sends the On-the-Way text from the car screen ([CarPlay](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004096735-How-ServiceM8-works-with-CarPlay)). Two-way SMS replies land in the job Diary and push the staff member ([Two-Way SMS](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001171696-How-to-use-ServiceM8-s-Two-Way-SMS)).

**Where it stops.** The customer gets no persistent "my job" page: Track My Arrival is "only available to customers while you are 'Navigating' to their job, and it switches off shortly after" ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/servicem8-add-ons/servicem8-add-ons/what-is-track-my-arrival)), so from arrival to invoice the customer sees nothing live — no confirmation the technician actually arrived, no lateness before the On-the-Way text, no ETA once navigation ends, no reschedule or cancel control. There are four job statuses only — Quote, Work Order, Completed, Unsuccessful — and Unsuccessful means "the quote was not accepted or the job has been cancelled" ([ServiceM8](https://support.servicem8.com/help-center/app/faq/what-does-job-status-mean)); searches of the help centre for "customer not home", "no show" and "late cancellation fee" found nothing, so a one-van business improvises with a custom badge, a Diary note, a door photo and a manual call-out invoice, none of it automated or customer-visible. Two-way SMS is a mobile web portal: each outgoing text ends with a "Reply here" link, and when the message already contains a Track My Arrival link the footer becomes "To reply, tap the link above" ([Two-Way SMS](https://support.servicem8.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001171696-How-to-use-ServiceM8-s-Two-Way-SMS)) — a 1-star review from 7 October 2026 complains that customers cannot text photos to the business number and nothing alerts the business to an incoming SMS, and another from 11 September describes a job "repeatedly delayed" with the notice arriving the Saturday before ([Trustpilot UK](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/servicem8.com)). The Trustpilot profile is **4.1** from 117 reviews with **20%** one-star, and the recurring themes are chat-only support, billing surprises, rejected feature requests and, until very recently, Android: a March 2026 UK reviewer called the Android app "pretty pointless", two Android stories later ServiceM8 shipped an "all-new, full-featured" one in September 2026 ([SoftwareSorted](https://softwaresorted.co.uk/electricians/tradify-vs-servicem8/); [September 2026 update](https://www.servicem8.com/september-2026-update)). No geofence radius, dwell time or force-quit behaviour is documented anywhere, so TurnUp should not quote figures for ServiceM8's detection.

**What the API lets a connector do.** REST is throttled at **180 requests a minute and 20,000 a day** per app per account ([HTTP response codes](https://developer.servicem8.com/docs/http-response-codes)). Webhooks cover nine objects including Job and Job Activity (the check-in record), fire when a subscribed field changes, and send only the object type, the UUID, the changed field names and a resource URL — the payload "does not contain the record itself or the new field values" — so every event costs one GET; the callback must return 2xx within **10 seconds**, failures are retried for **72 hours** then the subscription is cancelled, and a 429 throttles the account's webhooks for 15 minutes ([Webhooks overview](https://developer.servicem8.com/docs/webhooks-overview); [webhook subscription reference](https://developer.servicem8.com/reference/post_event_webhook_subscription)). The earlier formal-processes note described named events such as `job.checked_in`; the current developer documentation describes object-level field-change notifications instead, so the connector should key on Job Activity changes and Job status rather than named events. Public OAuth apps get scopes including `staff_locations` (real-time GPS, polled, no push stream), `publish_sms` (which "incurs account charges" against the customer's own allowance), `publish_diary_items` (immutable, attributed), `create_notifications` and `manage_badges`, and can add a Job Action button to the Job card on web and in the app — but it opens an iframe popup rather than a persistent panel, and there is no documented hook into the check-in button, the Navigate flow or the On-the-Way composer ([Authentication](https://developer.servicem8.com/docs/authentication); [Add-on Capabilities](https://developer.servicem8.com/docs/add-on-capabilities)). Nothing lets an integration fire a ServiceM8 Automation or edit the Track My Arrival page. Private add-ons via API key or the Addon Kit avoid Store review entirely, which is the fast path for a single-business pilot ([Addon Kit](https://developer.servicem8.com/docs/addon-kit)).

**What TurnUp builds now, prepares for, and does better.** Now: drive stages server-side from the webhook — Job Activity check-in to On site, check-out to Job done, status to Unsuccessful to Cancelled — and write a Diary item back for every TurnUp event so the trade's system of record has the evidence; send the TurnUp link through `publish_sms` on an OAuth app, or through TurnUp's own gateway once it has one; use `manage_badges` to create "Customer not home" and "Running late" badges so ServiceM8's Badge Follow Up automation can act on them. Prepare for: once TurnUp sends SMS itself, the Track My Arrival page becomes replaceable by a link that does not expire at arrival; once there is a native shell, the auto check-in moves from ServiceM8's app into TurnUp's and the connector becomes two-way; once payments exist, deposits at booking stop being a ServiceM8-Stripe-only feature. Better: a persistent page through Paused and Job done; an "On my way" that fires from the previous job's "Job done" rather than a separate Navigate tap; a lateness message that is automatic and concrete; a customer-not-home record in one tap; a confirmation that re-sends on every reschedule however the booking was moved; and a customer page that honestly reports "last updated" when the trade is in a dead zone instead of silently omitting the link.

## The rest of the market stops at a one-shot text

Across the UK and ANZ set, the realistic price band for a sole trader is **£18–£45** per user per month — Gas Engineer Software from £18, Powered Now Business £28 for one user, YourTradebase £29 flat, Trade2base Starter £29, Workever £29, Tradify Lite £34, Fergus from £39 — while Commusoft (four-licence minimum, 12-month contract, onboarding compulsory), Okappy (five-to-ten-user minimums paid yearly), Eworks Manager (£130+ per team), BigChange (quote only) and Payaca (now £299 a month for solar and heat-pump installers) are not priced for one van ([Tradify](https://www.tradifyhq.com/en-gb/pricing); [Powered Now](https://www.powerednow.com/pricing); [GES](https://www.gasengineersoftware.co.uk/pricing); [Trade2base](https://www.trade2base.com/pricing); [Commusoft](https://www.commusoft.co.uk/?p=103); [Payaca](https://payaca.com/pricing/uk)). SMS costs extra almost everywhere — Tradify **£0.10** a message, Fergus **£8 per 100** — which means a product bundling unlimited status messages would be noticed ([Tradify](https://www.tradifyhq.com/en-gb/pricing); [Fergus UK](https://fergus.com/uk/pricing/)). In North America the comparables are Jobber Core at **$49** monthly ($29 billed annually) for one user, Connect at $139 and Grow at $199, and Housecall Pro Basic at $59–$79 and Essentials at $149–$189, with no GBP list price published by any of the six ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/); [HCP pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)).

The customer-facing pattern is the same everywhere: the "on my way" moment is a single message, and the live map, where it exists, is short-lived and expensive. Tradify's "I'm on my way!" opens the phone's own texting app, "the text message is sent from your phone and not from Tradify", and it never appears in the Job Activity Log — so a dispute about whether the customer was told has no evidence trail — and Tradify's automated texts are explicitly one-way ([Tradify ETA help](https://help.tradifyhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/22417143010841-How-To-Send-an-ETA-Estimated-Time-of-Arrival-Text-Message); [Tradify text messages](https://help.tradifyhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/26130939769497)). Tradify also "does not work offline" at all ([Tradify FAQ](https://help.tradifyhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026848353-Does-Tradify-Work-Offline)). Powered Now's "On my way" runs a live-traffic Google Maps lookup and sends an ETA by email or text, with no map or link ([Powered Now](https://support.powerednow.com/en/knowledge/eta-/-arrival-assistant)). Commusoft's On the Way link carries the engineer's photo and ID credentials, adding a live map and ETA only with "dedicated GPS hardware, not mobile phones" ([Commusoft](https://www.commusoft.co.uk/?p=3474); [Commusoft tracking](https://www.commusoft.co.uk/jobs/real-time-vehicle-tracking/)); BigChange's ETA alert link opens a live map that shows "live updates if traffic delays change the arrival window", at the cost of an app that reviewers say "absolutely mullers your phone's battery" and a Trustpilot score around **1.9** driven by three-year contracts ([BigChange](https://www.bigchange.com/features/eta-alerts); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/bigchange-jobwatch/id1281529950); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bigchange.com?page=2)); Joblogic's portal shows "the engineer's current position while travelling", but the portal is Enterprise-tier ([Joblogic](https://www.joblogic.com/adsa/); [Joblogic pricing](https://www.joblogic.com/pricing/)). GES lists "Engineer On His Way" as "coming soon" ([GES](https://www.gasengineersoftware.co.uk/pricing)).

In North America the links are vehicle trackers, not phones, and they expire on arrival. Housecall Pro's On My Way text is a plain notification unless the account pays **$20 per vehicle per month** for Force Fleet Tracking hardware, at which point the link shows the van's location, plate, employee photos and a contact card, refreshes every 10 seconds, and "will expire after you or your tech pushes Start, Finish, Invoice/Pay, or after 2 hours (whichever comes first)" ([HCP Vehicle GPS](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/6060351-vehicle-gps-tracking-overview)). Jobber's native On My Way has the technician pick **5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 45 or 60** minutes and send a previewed text; its live-tracking link, added in August 2025, is "limited to US accounts on Connect or higher using the Force Fleet Tracking integration" and expires at visit completion or scheduled end ([Jobber Help](https://help.getjobber.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021265374); [Jobber product update](https://productupdates.getjobber.com/102703-force-fleet-tracking-integration-enhances-on-my-way-texts-with-live-tracking-links), 403 on fetch, from search excerpt). FieldPulse's phone-GPS Trip Tracking SMS "stays active for one hour after sending" and "must be sent manually, cannot be tied to automatic triggers" ([FieldPulse Help](https://help.fieldpulse.com/en/articles/2517252-customer-communications-mobile-app)). ServiceTitan's tracking page shows distance, ETA and a map only "if the service address is verified"; otherwise the customer sees the technician's name and the destination ([ServiceTitan Help](https://help.servicetitan.com/docs/enable-text-and-email-dispatch-notifications.md)). The industry default for the ETA is a technician-chosen number of minutes, not a computed one, which shifts accountability to the person rather than the software; computed ETAs appear only where a GPS feed and a verified destination both exist. Stop counts, queue position and "you are next" indicators are absent across all six — and for one van with a known day plan, that is a cheap differentiator nobody offers.

Windows are the scheduling primitive, and nobody reconciles them with the on-the-way text. Jobber's arrival windows run from **15 minutes to 4 hours**, either after the start time or centred on it, appear in confirmations, reminders and the client hub via a template variable — and are "not mentioned in the on-my-way text"; a community member reports reminders put the start time first and the window lower down so customers "often miss the window" ([Jobber arrival windows](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/arrival-windows/); [Jobber Community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/job-details-scheduling/client-reminders/8590)). ServiceTitan practitioners run two-hour windows with set first calls and tell customers "the windows are approximate and will be updated if the tech runs late" ([ServiceTitan Community](https://community.servicetitan.com/t5/Call-Booking/How-are-you-scheduling-calls/m-p/11796)). The enterprise suites add the one feature the small-business apps lack: Oracle Field Service lets an operator **lock a communicated ETA** so later re-sequencing does not silently change what the customer was told ([Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/field-service/fawmt/c-wmt-overview-use-communicated-windows.html)). Because every product treats lateness as "send the on-my-way text again with a bigger number", the customer sees two contradictory ETAs with no explanation; a page that shows the *revised* promise and marks it as revised is a visible improvement over all of them.

The exception vocabulary lives only in the enterprise-leaning UK products, and it is worth borrowing precisely. Commusoft's app offers Accept or Reject the night before, Travel (which fires the on-our-way SMS), Abort at three points with a reason and the job kept open, "No access" with questions and a mandatory photo "to document the access issue", and Leave with an "Is the job complete?" prompt; "Once marked as Arrived, the appointment cannot be edited or moved" ([Commusoft appointment statuses](https://help.commusoft.com/en/articles/216882-appointment-statuses)). Joblogic adds Abandon ("Logs a Revisit Required request"), No Access where "a reason for the no access must be provided", Leave Site which "pauses the job and reverts it to 'Accepted'", a per-visit status audit with timestamps, back-dating of status times with a warning, and "Make Private" attachments that never reach the customer ([Joblogic mobile guide](https://support.joblogic.com/docs/joblogic-mobile-user-guide)). Fergus's two-way SMS lands replies in the job's Notes & History and its job card shows "Schedule not sent" in red until the customer has been told ([Fergus SMS](https://help.fergus.com/en/articles/11174752-send-and-receive-sms); [Fergus schedule](https://help.fergus.com/en/articles/11980535-how-to-send-a-job-schedule-to-customers-in-fergus)). Housecall Pro's Job Scheduled notification "is re-sent if the time or assigned employee changes", the only automated reschedule message found in either region ([HCP notifications](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/357613-customer-notifications-overview)). Powered Now's portal "doesn't require your customer to invent a password" and lets them "see who is visiting" ([Powered Now v9](https://powerednow.com/powered-now-v9-introducing-our-new-customer-portal/)). Nobody — in any region — documents a no-show fee, a customer-not-home status visible to the customer, or an automated late-arrival text; Jobber's community workaround for a no-show fee is to swap the job's line items for a "cancellation fee" item and invoice by hand ([Jobber Community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/invoicing-getting-paid/deposit-for-cancellations/2521)), and Housecall Pro's advice is to "waive the fee once, and invite them to reschedule" ([HCP resource](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/last-minute-cancellations/)). Jobber's tipping feature is the one revenue idea worth noting: about **24%** of homeowners opted to tip in its first five months ([Total Landscape Care](https://www.totallandscapecare.com/technology/article/15292973/jobber-announces-new-tipping-and-referral-feature)).

For connectors beyond ServiceM8, the field narrows fast. Housecall Pro exposes a "job on my way" webhook, but its open API and Zapier are MAX-plan only ([HCP Zapier webhooks](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/5884151-how-to-use-zapier-with-webhooks); [HCP pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)); Jobber has GraphQL webhooks at application level, with the Visit object lagging 15–30 seconds after a status webhook ([Jobber Community](https://community.getjobber.com/discussions/integrations/any-integrations-that-add-internal-notes/1688/replies/10546)); Commusoft has HMAC-signed webhooks on job created, updated and deleted ([Commusoft developer](https://developer.commusoft.com/webhooks-2114965m0)); Joblogic has a public API. Tradify and YourTradebase publish no API, webhooks or Zapier app ([API Evangelist](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/tradify/); [Supergood](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/yourtradebase)), so for the two cheapest UK apps the only hook is Tradify's Google Calendar sync. Zapier polls every 15 minutes on free tiers, which is useless for arrival states. The order of connectors after ServiceM8 is therefore Google Calendar (lowest common denominator, reads the day plan for any trade), then Housecall Pro and Jobber, then Commusoft; the rest are manual.

Two things about the ordering are deliberate. Everything in Tier 1 and most of Tier 2 is web-feasible today and is itself job management: a window, a confirmation with terms, a reminder with replies, an ordered day with propagation, a status audit, and a diary written back to the system of record are the skeleton of a booking tool, assembled from the events the tracking link already generates. And the Tier 3 capabilities are ordered by what they unlock rather than by glamour: SMS first, because it closes the iOS push gap and makes every Tier 1 fallback real; payments second, because it converts the pre-agreed fee and the goodwill gesture from words into money; the native shell last, because geofencing is the most expensive capability, the one every vendor warns is fragile without Wi-Fi, and the one whose web approximation is already good enough to test the proposition.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the obvious framing. TurnUp's tracking link looked like a feature competitors would eventually match; in fact the market has converged on the opposite design — a text at departure and a map that dies at the door — because every vendor built the customer surface as an appendage of a technician-tracking product. The persistent page through On site, Paused and Job done is therefore not a nicety to be defended but the thing that lets TurnUp be the layer that handles the bad day, which no incumbent, including ServiceM8, has built. The bad day is also where the law sits: the booking confirmation is a durable medium under the Consumer Contracts Regulations, the no-access record is what makes a fee fair under the Consumer Rights Act, and the regulated sectors have already taught customers that a missed window produces an automatic consequence. A product that encodes those three facts in its defaults is doing compliance work for the trade that no £29-a-month app attempts.

The web-versus-native question resolves into a sequencing decision rather than a limitation to apologise for. Tap-time evidence with three timestamps and an honest "location not validated" flag is more robust in a Wiltshire dead zone than ServiceM8's detection, which quietly omits its link when the phone has no fix; prompted reconstruction gets most of the value of "looks like you arrived at 09:04" for the cost of remembering an earlier fix. What a browser cannot do — wake on arrival, trail in the background, push to an uninstalled iPhone — maps exactly onto the three capabilities to acquire, in the order SMS, payments, native shell, and the first of those is the only one the tracking link needs to pass its own test. The forty-item list exists so that when that test is passed, the booking tool is already half-built from the event log, and the geofence arrives as a suggestion with an undo rather than as a promise.
